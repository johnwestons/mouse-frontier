# Weapon workbench: real-world visual research

Reviewed October 2, 2026. This is visual reference for a fictional game mechanic, not a repair guide.

## Scope

The weapon catalog contains 83 player weapons. The approved [firearm identity catalog](../../../references/weaponRef/FIREARM_REFERENCE_CATALOG.md) determines the real-world appearance anchors, even where game names or old ammunition metadata are misleading. Individual components and visual sources are recorded in [firearm specifications](firearm-part-specs.json) and [hand-weapon specifications](hand-weapon-part-specs.json). Manufacturer and museum references identify recognizable components; the game artwork determines their materials and colors.

The manual says normal maintenance does not require taking the rifle apart and routes deeper disassembly to qualified service. |
| AR-style carbines | [Colt AR-15 manual](https://www.colt.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ar-15_semiautomatic_rifle__carbine.pdf) | The bolt-carrier group and charging handle are identifiable components. Their selected parts are respectively a piston carrier and a short gas-tube handguard. Each component fits its named game weapon only. |
| 1911-style and striker-fired pistols | [Springfield Armory 1911 manual and parts diagrams](https://support.springfield-armory.com/manuals/1911-manual) and [GLOCK owner’s manuals](https://us.glock.com/en/owners-resources/downloadable-materials) | A 1911-style parts view identifies the slide, barrel, recoil spring group, and frame; a GLOCK manual likewise lists slide, barrel, recoil spring assembly, and frame. Keep the silhouettes distinct by family. The diagram supports high-level part identification, not universal part interchangeability. |
| Bolt-action rifles | [MAUSER 98 Standard instructions and downloads](https://www.mauser.com/Services/Downloads/Bedienungsanleitungen/) | A bolt is a visible, model-specific assembly. Vintage Bolt-Action Rifle receives its own turned-down bolt, explicitly labelled for that weapon. |
| Revolvers | [Ruger New Bearcat instruction manual](https://ruger.com/dataProcess/manual/fetch.php/ms/0912/manual_0912.pdf) | This specific single-action design removes its cylinder for routine cleaning. Treat that as a family-specific visual cue, not a universal revolver teardown. |
| Traditional bows and crossbows | [Bear Archery care guidance](https://www.beararchery.com/pages/faq), [TenPoint inspection guidance](https://www.tenpointcrossbows.com/faqs/should-i-inspect-my-crossbow-each-time-i-plan-to-shoot-it/), and [TenPoint maintenance guidance](https://www.tenpointcrossbows.com/faqs/how-do-i-properly-maintain-my-string-and-cables/) | Strings and cables are visible wear components. TenPoint asks owners to inspect for fraying or broken strands and replace damaged or missing parts; its guidance refers maintenance to the model manual and service support. Traditional bows should read as string-and-limb assemblies, not as firearm-style field strips. |
| Axes and bladed melee weapons | [Gränsfors Bruk axe care](https://www.gransforsbruk.com/en/the-axe-guide/axe-care/axe-and-leather-care) and [Benchmade knife care](https://support.benchmade.com/hc/en-us/articles/15081262296091-Use-and-Care) | Show a whetstone, cloth, or a secured handle as appropriate to the weapon. Axe guidance specifically calls out checking the handle-to-head fit, sharpening, and handle replacement; a knife maker emphasizes cleaning, tuning, sharpening, and basic service. These weapons do not need a firearm-style exploded layout. |

## Design implications

1. **Separate routine service from major repair.** Routine care can be a short, low-cost pass represented by a rag, small brush, oil tin, and a tidy inspection panel. Serious damage opens a major-repair state that requires both scrap and a compatible, found component.
2. **Give every weapon exactly one unique component.** All 83 weapons have a dedicated item ID, compatibility entry and transparent sprite. Similar weapon families never share a usable component. A found component can only repair its named weapon; the workbench combines it with scrap when major repair is required.
3. **Use an orderly work surface.** Official field-strip drawings favor separated assemblies with space between them and small items arranged deliberately. In Mouse Frontier, use pixel-art trays or framed part slots instead of realistic piles of screws or a photorealistic gunsmith bench.
4. **Let the game abstraction be visibly a game.** The `ALIGNMENT CHECK` meter in [concept v2](weapon-repair-minigame-concept-v2.png) is a proposed timing interaction, not a literal real-world repair step. The interface should communicate the fiction with a brass target band, clear part slots, scrap cost, and a readable success window.
5. **Show what can and cannot be repaired at a glance.** A part can read `SERVICEABLE`, `FOUND`, or `MISSING`; if a critical part is missing, disable major repair and identify the compatible part the player needs. Critical parts should remain world-found items, as requested, rather than craftable recipes.
6. **Preserve the game’s authored look.** Use the existing weapon sprites, Courier Prime type, dark brown panels, wood/brass frame art, cream labels, and muted green/red status colors. Keep detail chunky and readable at the game’s 960×720 canvas size. The concept uses those existing assets and colors.

## Limits of this research

The major-part requirement is a game abstraction. A weapon's single durability value represents overall wear and a failed fitted component, rather than a literal fracture of every surface. Leather grips do not imply that a snapped forged pry bar or knuckle body can be replaced by a grip. Fantasy weapons retain visible construction from their approved art: the boomerang joining plate, greatsword reinforcement plate and shock-baton contacts adapt recognizable component shapes to those fictional designs. No real-world repair procedure, dimensions or fabrication specification is implemented.
