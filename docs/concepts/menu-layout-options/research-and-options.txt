MOUSE FRONTIER: MENU LAYOUT OPTIONS
Research and concept illustrations, 2026-10-19.
Prepared for user selection. No gameplay code or saves changed in this design task.
Mockups created with the built-in image_gen tool. The adjacent image-prompts.json
records the complete prompt set and source screenshot. They illustrate proposed
interfaces; generated scenery and icons are not production replacement assets.

CURRENT LAYOUT FINDINGS
The host/single-player mobile Journey Menu is a large central panel. It mixes
Travel, Backpack, Map, Upgrades, Move/Scale, Maintenance, Poses, Pause, Leave
Train and Journal. Poses and Leave Train sit below less frequent train tools.
A specific pose requires MENU > CHARACTER POSES > pose: three taps.
Leave Train requires MENU > LEAVE TRAIN: two taps when available.
Departing requires MENU > TRAVEL > confirmation: three taps when affordable.
Mobile has a separate direct Backpack button, so burying Backpack in More is
a real tradeoff in option B.
Leave Train is rendered only on the stopped train in car 1. Its disappearance
makes availability and location harder to understand.
Desktop has different compact and side-panel arrangements. Co-op guests have
another right-hand list with Poses, Map, Journal, Workshop, Decorate and
conditional travel-related actions. Unify naming, order and navigation while
retaining the existing co-op permissions and requests.
The reference screenshot has two tall status rows. All three concepts use a
compact shared status strip so the alternatives can be compared fairly.

OPTIONS
A. RADIAL QUICK MENU (a-radial-menu.png)
One Actions launcher opens six broad fixed wedges: Poses, Leave Train, Travel,
Backpack, Map and More. Center cancels. Poses opens four choices.
Best for a clear resting screen and learned directional shortcuts.
Ordinary tap navigation still takes three taps for a pose and two for Leave;
the benefit is shorter search/reach, not a guaranteed reduction in taps.
Use tap-open/tap-choice by default. Optional press-drag-release can be added
after testing. An edge launcher needs an inward fan or correctly anchored
gesture geometry; do not relocate a wheel under a stationary finger and
accidentally select a different wedge. Avoid multiple concentric action rings.

B. QUICK DOCK + POSE WHEEL (b-quick-dock-pose-wheel.png)
Persistent Poses, Leave Train, Travel and More buttons near the right thumb.
Poses opens a four-choice wheel: Stand, Sit, Lay Down, Use / Action.
Best fit for the user's stated priority: pose selection in two taps, Leave
in one tap when available, departure in two taps including confirmation.
Tradeoffs: more persistent screen coverage and Backpack moves to More.
Keep normal tap selection and center cancel. The dock could later allow
user-selected shortcuts if keeping direct Backpack access proves important.

C. LARGE-BUTTON DRAWER (c-action-drawer.png)
One Actions tab opens a compact two-column, three-row labeled tile grid.
Poses expands within this panel with a clear Back control.
Best for large labels, predictable scanning and simple discrete input.
Tradeoff: two taps for Leave, three for a pose; the open panel covers part
of the scene. No scrolling required for the six primary choices.

RECOMMENDATION
Prototype B first for fastest access to the user's named actions. A is the
strongest alternative if minimizing permanent HUD clutter matters more.
C is a useful large-button option. This is a design judgment based on the
current flow and cited guidance, not a claim that these concepts have already
won a phone usability study.

COMMON BEHAVIOR FOR THE SELECTED DESIGN
- Pair simple icons with short horizontal labels; avoid icon-only guessing.
- Keep stable slots. Show unavailable train actions dimmed with a reason
  such as Train moving or Go to front car, instead of silently removing them.
- Use a consistent exit/return slot at stops and caravans with explicit labels.
  Preserve existing rules, events and host/guest progression authority.
- Travel opens the existing cost review. Departure still needs confirmation.
- Put upgrades, maintenance, decorating, journal and settings in a grouped
  secondary panel. Keep a direct Pack shortcut if chosen during prototype.
- Make touch targets at least 48 by 48 Android dp on the device; the game's
  logical canvas pixels are not automatically dp. Use larger targets where
  space permits, visible separation and existing adjustable control sizing.
- Support tap navigation, clear cancel/back and digital controller focus.
  Drag gestures are optional shortcuts. Select on release with cancellation.
- Keep controls out of cutouts, system gesture areas, movement and USE hit
  regions. Preserve control customization and offer mirrored placement.
- The compact strip is a proposal; full resources, ammo, XP and other stats
  must remain accessible in an expanded status view. Combat retains the
  stats necessary for that mode.
- Treat menus as input layers so opening/selecting cannot also move, fire or
  activate objects behind them. Preserve the existing multiplayer pause model.
- First-person minigames retain their dedicated aim/fire controls; do not
  place the exploration dock or wheel on top of the new aiming joystick.
- Validate the chosen layout on a small landscape phone, wide phone, tablet
  and desktop/controller. Compare time, wrong selections and finger reach
  for Sit, Leave Train, Travel, Backpack and Cancel in host and guest roles.
  Check train moving, wrong car, insufficient supplies and blocked departures.

RESEARCH BASIS (primary sources)
Don Hopkins, The Design and Implementation of Pie Menus (1991):
https://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/piemenus/ddj/piemenus.html
Broad wedges close to an origin reduce pointing precision demands and can
support learned directions. Stable item positions matter. The article also
describes screen-space costs, difficulties with long labels and edge fans.
Its historical experiments do not establish the fastest layout for this
particular phone game.

Valve, Steamworks Radial Menus:
https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller/radial_menus?l=english
Shows a real game-input system with radial icons, several activation modes
and an explicit center-cancel pattern. Use that interaction pattern as a
reference, not as evidence that controller geometry transfers unchanged
to a touchscreen.

Microsoft, Xbox Accessibility Guideline 112, UI navigation:
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112
Consistent order, predictable focus, stable navigation conventions and a
clear route back inform all three concepts.

Microsoft, Xbox Accessibility Guideline 107, Input:
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107
Provide discrete input alternatives to gestures and prolonged holds.
Release activation and a way to cancel reduce accidental commitments.
This supports tap navigation even when a radial gesture is also available.

Android Developers, Make apps more accessible:
https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps
Recommends minimum 48 by 48 dp touch targets. Treat this as a device-space
minimum, then verify actual reach and clarity on target phones.
