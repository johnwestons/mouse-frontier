# Homes, furnishings, and resident routines

House loot is stored in the cupboards and shelves painted into each interior. All 250 furnished stop interiors have individually reviewed image coordinates in `game/house_storage.lua`. The interaction marker follows the same image transform as the house renderer. E, mouse, and touch open the storage; the painted fixture cannot be picked up or moved.

Generated furniture is removed when an existing home is prepared. Its stored contents, persistent item identities, and player-dropped belongings are retained. Empty legacy interior backgrounds resolve to a furnished image for that stop. Unvisited reward caches do not create partial stop layouts. Storage inspection remains read-only during co-op authorization, and reward overflow creates another fixed storage compartment.

The 37 furnishings in `game/furniture_catalog.lua` are purchased from ordinary traders and remain usable in train cars. Traders receive four location-based furniture offers, priced from 5 to 24 scrap before existing modifiers. A versioned stock update adds these once to existing traders without replenishing sold items. Existing owned furniture and starter train equipment remain usable.

Residents have one persistent actor, one assigned home, and a saved position in either that home or its stop. Each resident chooses a different order of sweeping, transporting supplies, and tending a work spot, with one to three chores per outing. Initial activity is staggered by 8–50 seconds; outdoor chores have 18–45-second breaks, and home rests last 40–110 seconds. Individual chore durations also vary. The last outdoor chore cannot repeat immediately, and completed chores have an 85–160-second cooldown. Loading and delivering supplies stay together as one task. Nearby crates and work props supply context where available; reachable home and neighboring porch locations supply fallbacks. Routines use the stop walkmask and save each resident's random stream, chosen activity, delays, and cooldowns, so reloads and interaction pauses preserve the schedule. Recruited passengers leave the resident roster.

The host owns simulation and interaction reach checks. Guests display private movement interpolation while retaining authoritative actor positions for actions. Multiple residents may be outside together; a player can talk or trade with the resident they approach independently of the other player's selection. The scripted starting-home scenes retain their existing story behavior.

The character roster receives three new authored actions: sweeping, stationary crate lifting/lowering, and tending with a cup and cloth. Existing directional walking remains driven by actual movement distance. Transported supplies attach to the backpack during walking. Chore art uses four 512×256 frames with a shared body scale and foot anchor; stationary tasks use the authored orientation without mirroring costume details.

Art was generated with the built-in ImageGen tool. Runtime strips are `assets/sprites/character-animations/<character>/{sweep,carry,tend}.png`. The final prompts and source mappings are in `character-motion/chores/generation-prompts.json`; reviewed hashes and audit evidence are in `character-motion/chores/acceptance.json`. Original/staged sources and composited review sheets remain under `output/resident-chores`.

Validation includes furniture purchase/placement/save behavior, all interior anchor mappings, storage migration and non-pickup enforcement, co-op recipient access, resident save/resume/door occupancy, full chore cycles on all 50 settlement walkmasks, animation scale/cadence, and streamed family retention. `tools/npc-life-smoke/run.ps1 -Mode both` exercises the real application in desktop and mobile layouts, including touch storage interaction. Captures are in `.stabilization/npc-life`; storage anchor contact sheets are in `.stabilization/house-storage-audit`.

No new character dialogue was added. Development verification uses isolated/in-memory fixtures; existing user saves did not require a reset.

October 20, 2026: permanent identities and named tasks (stages 1 and 2)

`game/npc_identity.lua` separates each resident from its sprite. Campaign-local `npcIdentities` records store a permanent ID, full name, species, occupation, home stop and door. The conductor is Silas Reed and the home-yard mechanic is Tobin Flint. Ordinary residents receive distinct full names from a curated 1,600-name pool, selected without consuming gameplay random rolls and persisted after allocation. A resident at another stop using the same artwork is a separate person. Existing caravan merchant names retain their existing identities and presentation. Personality-driven behavior and new character stories remain deferred.

Residents and passengers carry `characterId`. Personal chatter history, gifts, aid, trade terms and rides resolve that individual. Passenger selection retains the chosen ID when several passengers share artwork; gifts and equipment go to the selected person. Boarding, arrival contributions, arrival rewards and saved completion notices retain their name. The permanent record keeps their last arrival stop after disembarkation. Existing chore timing and approved character speech remain unchanged.

`game/npc_targets.lua` reserves a specific person when accepting mail or supply work. Both task types store `senderId` and `recipientId`, alongside legacy asset references. Preparation honors reserved residents and home doors. An old letter naming an absent resident adds that person to the saved roster while retaining existing actors and routine progress. Hand-ins require the named person and live reach/room checks; another critter at the same stop cannot accept their delivery. A recipient with unfinished deliveries cannot become a passenger until those deliveries are completed.

Conversation speakers, trading posts, gift confirmations, passenger notices, tactical ally names and host/guest journal entries use the same saved names. Journal cards include the existing critter portrait, named recipient, named sender and destination. Right-side identity cards show species, occupation and directions. Directions update between the station and the actual occupied home; gold rings and delivery icons mark the recipient or their doorway. The trail map includes the current recipient's name and directions. Character labels no longer float over the walking scene. The shared UI reads private accessibility preferences without modifying a network replica.

Save schema and network state manifest advance together to version 46. Existing active deliveries acquire named reservations without replaying rewards. Legacy sprite-wide relationship history is retained and associated with at most one known person; it is never copied to every resident using that sprite. The identity registry and task references replicate from the host. Rendering and guest journal models do not allocate names or change assignments.
