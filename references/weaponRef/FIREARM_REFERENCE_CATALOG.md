# Shooting Range Firearm Reference Catalog

Last updated: 2026-08-30

This catalog locks the real-world construction, sight geometry, and reference hierarchy for the first-person shooting-range sprites. The existing inventory art remains the authority for each weapon's decorative finish, repairs, wraps, and wasteland character. Real firearm references are the authority for anatomy, moving-part relationships, sight alignment, and first-person perspective.

Bulk sprite generation stays on hold until the identity and geometry in this file are accepted. A clean-looking image is not enough if the action, stock, barrel, or sights are physically inconsistent.

## Locked first-person rules

- Every ranged weapon receives one transparent-background hip-fire sprite and one transparent-background ADS sprite.
- No hands or arms appear in either sprite.
- The entire weapon remains inside the asset canvas with deliberate transparent padding. Nothing is pre-cropped for the game viewport.
- ADS is a true shooter-eye view. The bore, receiver, grip or stock, rear sight, and front sight remain on one centerline.
- With a notch sight, the top of the front post is level with the rear sight shoulders and centered with equal light on both sides.
- With an aperture sight, the front-post tip is centered in the aperture. With an MP5 diopter, the hooded front post is centered inside the selected rear drum aperture.
- A shotgun keeps the sight system shown by its base weapon. Bead-only barrels do not gain a fake rear notch; slug/deer barrels with an adjustable rear and ramped front blade keep that open-sight pair.
- The reticle anchor is the point of aim at the top-center of the aligned front post or the center of the bead, not the geometric center of the PNG.
- Long-gun stocks do not turn sideways in ADS. Butt, wrist, receiver, barrel, and muzzle converge naturally along the viewing axis.
- ADS has zero yaw and zero roll. Detachable magazines and pistol grips stay directly beneath the receiver centerline and appear edge-on with strong foreshortening; their broad side profiles may not project left or right.
- Levers remain beneath the receiver and wrist. Magazines, charging handles, ejection ports, cylinders, hammers, hinges, and wraps may not occupy impossible locations or block required movement.
- Pixel art may simplify texture, but it may not simplify away the defining geometry of the model.

## Reference priority

1. User photographs of the exact firearm.
2. Manufacturer product photographs, manuals, and catalogs.
3. Museum collections and government technical manuals.
4. Reputable secondary photographs only when the first three do not show a required angle.
5. The existing game sprite for finish, wear, cloth, tape, and fictional alterations.

## Reference coverage and photo request list

The five supplied rear views are accepted and preserved. For any additional user-owned model, the most useful optional set is:

1. Full left profile.
2. Full right profile.
3. Straight top view showing sight spacing and receiver width.
4. Straight rear view centered on the bore.
5. True sight picture with rear and front sights aligned.
6. Receiver/action close-up.
7. For long guns, a full shooter-eye view that includes the butt or stock, as in the Marlin photo.
